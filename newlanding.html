This project starts from a simple difficulty: understanding is never produced from a completely neutral or complete description. We reason from limited memories, partial observations, inherited representations and locally shared ways of making sense.

Rather than treating these limitations simply as defects to be eliminated, the project asks how they participate in the conditions under which something can become sufficiently stable to be distinguished, represented, explored and perhaps reproduced.

The project is not a finished theory.
It is a collection of constructions, experiments, mathematical ideas, conceptual fragments and formal developments. They explore what may become stable under different ways of looking, describing, interrupting and reconstructing something. Some directions may connect; others may remain distinct.

Why this work?

Understanding rarely starts from a complete description. It can emerge by carrying traces across interruptions, reconstructing what is missing, and discovering what remains stable when the reconstruction changes.

What appears to us as a point, a boundary or a common object may itself be the result of several contextual views becoming sufficiently compatible. The project therefore explores not only representations, but the conditions under which something can become stable enough to be treated as one.

This includes questions about observers, communities, language, memory, computation and mathematics. The mathematical languages appearing in the project are therefore not taken as a single final language: they are among the structures through which distinctions can become stable, and through which previously implicit distinctions may become visible again.

This connects to questions arising in quantum information, where what can be said about a system may depend essentially on the context in which it is described.

From fragments to constructions

The different pages should not be read as a linear theory. They are different attempts to stabilize related questions. An assemblage may suggest a mathematical construction; a mathematical construction may reveal a limitation of the original intuition; a physical or computational example may in turn reopen the question.

The resulting structure is less an ordered tree than a forest of interpretations: some paths meet, some diverge, and some become temporarily more stable than others.

The order presented here is therefore mainly an order of exploration, not a claim that one branch is the origin or destination of the others.

Current state

This is an ongoing, open and deliberately revisable project. Some pages are relatively stable; others are exploratory. They should be read with different expectations.

At present, the project has no single final language. Some constructions are becoming more stable; others remain deliberately open so that their interpretation can still be interrupted and reconsidered. Moving between languages may itself reveal a different structure.

In particular, the assemblages preserve ideas precisely at the stage where their interpretation is not yet fixed. They are part of the project, not an archive of discarded material.
