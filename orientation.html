Orientation

Identity as a community process
with its two inherent directions

This page provides an orienting access to the project. It is not a foundation, nor a doctrine, but an invitation to a particular mode of reading.

Identity as a community process

This manifesto primarily seeks to redefine itself. It addresses anthropomorphic processes that experience the “possibility of perfection” as a legitimate and meaningful question. Whether their beliefs lean toward affirmation or negation matters little; whether they identify first as mathematicians, theologians, biologists, poets, or philosophers matters little as well. On the contrary, such plurality can only enrich the community that may form around this work.

Rather than postulating the existence of fundamental, non-decomposable symbolic entities governing a non-composable universe of which we are a part, this work begins from a different stance. Letters are treated as primitive, non-decomposable gestures only relative to a given frame. Lexical, semantic, and interpretative constructions remain processes: historically situated, interruptible, and revisable. Meaning does not arise from immutable atoms, but from the synchronization of construction processes operating at different scales.

Reading and writing are not treated as symmetric or isolated acts. Writing projects a trajectory; reading unfolds another. Understanding emerges from their partial synchronization, as a superposition of possible paths constrained by shared stabilizations. What appears as a rigid derivation may thus be understood as a family of synchronized interpretations, rather than as a single analytical unfolding.

Scientific rigor plays a crucial role in enabling such synchronizations by providing stable frames of reference. Yet, like any form of rigidification, it simultaneously secures and constrains imagination by favoring pre-established formal routes. These stabilizations are not rejected here; they are treated as necessary local agreements that allow communities to act, reason, and remember together.

Changes of scale or conceptual reorientations are rare in the history of human communities, often because implicit conventions of measurement and interpretation remain unnoticed. What appears as continuity may thus result less from necessity than from the persistence of shared frames that make alternative trajectories harder to perceive.

From this perspective, the richness of linguistic polysymy— such as that found in Eastern languages or in the dual ordering of the Arabic alphabet— becomes a resource rather than an obstacle. Translation is not secondary, but primitive: it is through translation that meaning is carried, displaced, synchronized, and enriched within and across communities.

Understanding without closure

To understand without betrayal, it must always remain possible to complete a given situation with an indefinable center. Such a center is not an object, nor a hidden variable, but a condition of intelligibility: it is postulated in order to orient understanding, yet never fully determined.

An epistemic position may be expressed locally as a tuple (d, q, p, b). This tuple should not be read as an absolute coordinate system, but as a situated stance relative to a presumed center. Its components acquire meaning only through this relation.

Being positioned at d does not uniquely determine the structure of the surrounding space. It may correspond either to an open quarter-plane, preserving interpretative freedom, or to the estimation of sub-central boundaries introduced to obtain a bounded RTM. These boundaries do not define the center itself; they are provisional constraints enabling computation, communication, or collective synchronization.

In this sense, rigidification is not the closure of meaning, but the local stabilization of relations. A bounded RTM remains compatible with an unbounded center, provided that the possibility of revisiting or relaxing boundaries is preserved.

On plurality, translation, and reality

A human community may sustain a shared sense of reality even when multiple, conceptually distinct interpretations coexist. What matters is not the elimination of alternatives, but the possibility of translating between them under common observational constraints.

A simple illustration is provided by many-worlds interpretations in quantum mechanics. When different models yield identical predictions once observations are described within the same framework, no epistemic necessity enforces the selection of a single interpretation. In such cases, Occam’s razor is not required: the models can be superposed rather than reduced.

This plurality is not a weakness. On the contrary, it enables modulation of information across interpretations. Diversity of models enriches the community by offering multiple encoding and translation pathways, increasing robustness without sacrificing synchronization.

First intention

The text below is presented as a first intention. It precedes the stabilized orientation above and is kept here as part of the construction process.

L’identité comme processus communautaire : Ce manifeste cherche principalement à se redéfinir et s’adresse principalement aux processus anthropomorphes qui ressentent « la possibilité de la perfection » comme questionnement légitime et intéressant. Qu’ils aient des croyances qui penchent vers le oui ou le non importe peu, qu’ils se sentent d’abord mathématiciens, théologiens, biologistes, poètes ou philosophes importe peu, au contraire cela ne peut qu’enrichir la communauté qui peut se construire dessus.

A priori il me semble moins accessible tel qu’il est à un Scientifique (je veux dire ici une instance de la représentation par défaut que je me fais d' un processus anthropomorphe dont je sais juste qu’il représente un scientifique qui lit ces lignes par rapport à celui dont je ne sais rien), parce que même s’il y a des notions d’univalence en logique, le Scientifique n’a ni le courage du Poète ni l’innocence de l’Enfant : sa rigueur caractéristique du lien communautaire est une corde qui assure mais aussi une chaîne qui tend à restreindre l’imaginaire par le forçage du rentrer dans le cadre des formalisations préétablies.

Plutôt que de postuler l’existence de processus fondamentaux nommables avec des symboles non décomposables qui régissent un univers (non composable) dont on fait partie (le lecteur et moi et d’autres choses comme existences), il me semble plus raisonnable de commencer par voir ce qui se passe si on ne fige a priori ni un bord ni l’autre ni même la conscience de soi et de non-soi de celui qui parle.

Souvent on s’intéresse à des percepts locaux objectifiés vus avec une méthode rigidifiée. Cela fait sens car les changements d’échelle ou remises en cause dogmatiques sont souvent exceptionnels dans les histoires de communautés humaines, un peu comme si on collait des espaces tangents par adjonction par défaut, faute de conscience de la convention de mesure implicite que l’on a prise ensemble pour se rassurer que l’on parle des mêmes choses.

Les processus d’émergence ou de déclin sont souvent étudiés avec une clé d’accès rigidifiée, comme si les autres (souvent incompatibles) n’existaient pas. On a ainsi souvent tendance à rigidifier l’histoire, par exemple à travers des causalités de type bouc émissaire dans les récits de fondation ou de déclin moral.

La polysémie des langues orientales ou la dualité même de l’ordre alphabétique en arabe sont une richesse, car elles incitent à penser la traduction comme primitive, capable de porter et d’enrichir le sens.
